Years of experience don't make someone senior.

Five years of the same work is one year, five times.

What actually changes from junior to mid to senior:
[images: 1]
---
It's not how much code you know. It's how big a problem you can own without someone checking on you.

Junior: owns a task.
Mid: owns a feature.
Senior: owns a problem or a system.
---
The same bug, three ways:

Junior fixes the line that crashed.
Mid fixes the cause and adds a test.
Senior asks why this kind of bug keeps happening, and changes the design so it stops.
[images: 2]
---
Seniority is visible in the work you prevent.

"We don't need a new service, a database index is enough" can save weeks. Write those decisions down. Nobody sees work that didn't happen.
---
Junior owns a task, mid owns a feature, senior owns the outcome.
