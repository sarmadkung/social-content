Years of experience do not make someone senior. Five years of the same work is one year, five times.

## What the levels really mean

The levels are not about how much code you know. They are about how big a problem you can own without someone checking on you.

Think of learning to cook. First you follow a recipe exactly. Then you cook a full meal alone. Later you plan the menu for a wedding and know which dishes can go wrong.

## Why it matters

If you think seniority means knowing more frameworks, you will learn frameworks and stay stuck. The real change is in **scope** and judgement. Scope is how much you are trusted to own. A junior might own one ticket; a senior might own the whole checkout system.

## What changes at each level

![Junior owns a task, mid-level owns a feature, senior owns a problem: what each one does](1.png)

- **Junior: owns a task.** Gets a clear ticket, asks for help and learns the codebase. Success means working code that passes review.
- **Mid-level: owns a feature.** Turns a vague request into tasks, finishes without much help, and spots edge cases. An edge case is a rare input that breaks things, like a user who leaves their name empty.
- **Senior: owns a problem or a system.** Asks "should we build this at all?", sees risks early, and makes the whole team faster.

## The same bug, three ways

![The same bug fixed by a junior, a mid-level and a senior, and what each fix stops](2.png)

Take one bug: the app crashes when a user's name is empty.

- **Junior:** fixes the line that crashed. That stops this one crash.
- **Mid-level:** fixes the cause and adds a test. That stops this bug for good.
- **Senior:** asks why this kind of bug keeps happening, and changes the process or design so it stops. For example, every input gets checked where it enters the system. That stops every bug like it.

## What does not change

- You still read code, debug and write tests every week.
- You still ask questions. Seniors just ask them earlier.
- Nobody knows everything at any level.

## For mid-levels aiming higher

Seniority is visible in the work you prevent. A senior who says "we don't need a new service, a database index is enough" just saved weeks. Write those decisions down, because nobody sees work that did not happen.

## Keep in mind when you aim for the next level

- Ask your lead what the next level means on your team. It differs from team to team.
- Take the vague ticket. Owning the unclear part is how scope grows.
- Ask "what problem does this solve?" before you build, at any level.
- Count reviews, docs and unblocking others as real work.

**In one line:** junior owns a task, mid owns a feature, senior owns the outcome.
