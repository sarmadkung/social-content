TITLE: Seniority grows by scope, not years of experience
COVER: cover.png
---
Years of experience do not make someone senior. Five years of the same work is one year, five times. The levels are about how big a problem you can own without someone checking on you, not how much code you know.

- **Junior owns a task.** A clear ticket, help when stuck, learning the codebase. Done means working code that passes review.
- **Mid-level owns a feature.** Turns a vague request into tasks, finishes without much help, and spots edge cases: the rare inputs that break things.
- **Senior owns a problem or a system.** Asks "should we build this at all?", sees risks early, and makes the whole team faster.

The same bug shows the difference. A junior fixes the line that crashed. A mid-level fixes the cause and adds a test. A senior asks why this kind of bug keeps happening and changes the process or design so it stops.

**What does not change:** you still read code, debug and write tests every week, and you still ask questions. Seniors just ask them earlier.

**Seniority is visible in the work you prevent.** "We don't need a new service, a database index is enough" can save weeks. Write those decisions down, because nobody sees work that did not happen.

Junior owns a task, mid owns a feature, senior owns the outcome.
